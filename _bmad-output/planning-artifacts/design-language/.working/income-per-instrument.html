The question being decided

Should /income break income down per instrument, and if so, how? Today the page answers how much came in, and when (annual bars, year × month matrix) and who paid the most, all time (top-5 list, per-position table). It does not answer how one instrument's income developed over time, nor what a given year is made of.

Available data is unchanged in every option: Portfolixir.Portfolios.Income already returns every dividend and interest entry with security_id, date, gross, withheld tax and net. No new query, no new source. What differs is presentation cost and what each presentation hides.

Constraints every option is drawn under: server-rendered inline SVG (no bundler, no charting library), one accent active at a time, chart data always also reachable as a table (UX-DR10).

My pick

Selection is a note to self; nothing is stored. Answering with the ID alone is enough.

I1 Sparkline column in the existing per-position table

Answers
Per instrument: is its income rising, flat, falling, or a one-off spike — without leaving the table that already carries gross / tax / net.
Build cost
Lowest. One extra <td> and a ~15-line polyline helper in IncomeLive; the per-year values are one group_by over data already loaded. No new viewBox conventions, no legend, no interaction.
Drawback
The sparkline is unlabelled and unscaled: each row is normalised to its own maximum, so Nordwerk's 1.800,00 spike and Helio's 120,00 look equally tall. It shows shape, never magnitude, and rows with one or two payments degenerate — see the last two rows.

I2 Stacked bars, top 6 plus „Sonstige“

Answers
What a year is made of, and how that composition shifts between years — the 2023 spike is visibly one instrument, not a good year.
Build cost
Highest of the four. The existing bar chart is a stretched viewBox="0 0 n 100" with preserveAspectRatio="none"; textures and axis text cannot survive that distortion, so this chart needs its own undistorted viewBox, its own axis/label rendering, a top-N + rest aggregation in the context, and a legend component. Roughly a day, most of it in Elixir, not CSS.
Drawback
Seven segments is past the readable limit. Zinsen in 2022 is 2,5 px tall — present, countable in the table, but not comparable by eye. And „Sonstige“ here totals 1.670,00, more than four of the six named segments: the bucket that exists to reduce noise is itself a large unexplained block.
Colour constraint
The one-accent rule does fight this. Six tints of a single hue are not six distinguishable colours, especially at 2–20 px segment heights. The attempt below adds a second, non-colour channel: every second segment carries an SVG texture (diagonal, dots, cross-hatch) over its tint, and „Sonstige“ uses the neutral text-muted mix rather than a seventh tint. It is honest to say this works for the top three or four segments and stops working below that — verify by switching the accent to teal and coral above, where the tint ramp compresses further.

I3 Instrument filter on the existing charts

Answers
One instrument, or a small named set, against the same time axis — and against the unfiltered total, which stays visible as a ghost bar behind the selection.
Build cost
Medium, and almost none of it is chart work. The SVG is unchanged: same viewBox, same preserveAspectRatio="none", same rects, one extra ghost rect per bar. The cost is LiveView state: a selection assign, phx-click per chip, and the URL parameter needed for a filtered view to survive a reload.
Drawback
It only answers questions already asked. Composition is invisible unless every instrument is clicked in turn, and with ten instruments the chip row is already two lines — at fifty it needs the search field to be more than decoration.

I4 No per-instrument view at all (control)

Answers
Who paid the most, all time, top five — and, via the per-position table below it, the same for every instrument with gross, withheld tax, net, payment count and last payment date.
Build cost
Zero. This is what /income renders today, unchanged.
Drawback
Every figure is an all-time total, so nothing about development is visible: a holding sold in 2023 and a holding bought in 2025 sit in the same ranking with no way to tell them apart, and the 2023 spike is invisible because Nordwerk's special dividend is folded into one number.

Notes on this file

  • All securities, amounts and dates are invented. Round figures are deliberate so no reading of this page can be mistaken for a real position.
  • Tokens, .data-table, .workspace-section, .contributor-list, .area-tab, .hint and the .income-* rules are copied from priv/static/app.css. New rules are marked NEW in the stylesheet and are the actual CSS cost of each option.
  • app.css has no generic .data-table .num rule: the live income table already marks money cells class="num", but nothing right-aligns them. This page adds that rule; shipping any option means adding it for real.
  • Every chart carries role="img" with a figure-bearing aria-label and is duplicated as a table — inline for I1 and I4, behind a <details> disclosure for I2 and I3 (UX-DR10).
  • Theme follows prefers-color-scheme until the toggle sets data-theme, which then wins in both directions. The accent switch exists so the I2 tint ramp can be checked against all three accents, not only violet.
  • Transitions are the only motion and sit inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference).
  • No external requests: no CDN, no web font, no image. Inter and JetBrains Mono are replaced by the system stack, so line lengths and digit widths differ slightly from the running app.
